**Case Study: 3 Routed Commercial Introductions for RESPILON in \~43 Days**

**3 demand-side introductions routed in \~43 days for RESPILON, a technical nanofiber company — built from signals, not *spray-and-pray*.**

**RESPILON** operates in a technical market where generic lead generation is frankly weak.

The company’s core edge is nanofiber technology, with possible applications across filtration, protective materials, health, pharma, defence, industrial R\&D, and other high-value use cases. That kind of offer has a wide market surface, but that also creates the main commercial problem: not every “relevant” company is actually worth a conversation.

**A broad list would create noise. The route had to be built around signals.**

Instead of treating the market as one big pool, we looked for companies where RESPILON’s technology had a believable commercial reason to matter. That meant looking at signals like category fit, product surface, technical use case, industrial relevance, filtration/protection needs, innovation/R\&D activity, and whether the company had a reason to explore advanced material or nanofiber-based solutions.

The goal was not to “get replies.” The goal was to find places where the introduction could create economic movement. Over roughly 43 days, the route produced **3 qualified introductions**. The ROI logic was simple. In a technical B2B market like this, one relevant commercial conversation can be worth far more than the cost of sourcing the route. If even one introduction turns into a pilot, distribution conversation, technical evaluation, supply agreement, or commercial partnership, the upside can justify the entire campaign.

That is why the model is not measured like normal outreach. A generic cold email campaign asks: “How many replies did we get?” A routed commercial system asks: “Did we create conversations that could realistically turn into revenue, partnerships, or strategic accounts?”

For RESPILON, the result was 3 introductions in \~43 days, built through signal-led routing rather than volume-based lead generation. This is the core proof: we did not sell a list, and we did not spray the market. We identified where RESPILON’s technology had commercial relevance, filtered the market through buying/use-case signals, and routed the company into conversations that had a reason to exist.

That is controlled commercial routing.
